The image is 224 by 224 pixels, and comes with no clinical context, no patient information and no scale bar: judge only what this image shows.

Answer all 9 questions below about what is visible in this image. Each question lists its levels in order. For every question choose the one level that best fits this image.

1. nucleus_present
   Does the cell in the centre of the image contain a nucleus, that is a distinct darker internal body, or is the whole cell body uniform with no nucleus at all?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     absent: the cell body is one even mass throughout, with no darker internal patch standing out from the rest
     present: a distinctly darker, sharply bounded internal body sits inside the cell and contrasts with the paler cytoplasm

2. cell_size
   How large is the central cell compared with the surrounding red blood cells: clearly smaller than one red cell, about the same size, or clearly larger?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     much_smaller: the cell spans about a quarter of a neighbouring red cell, near 2 um across
     similar: the cell matches a nearby red cell in width to within about a fifth of that width
     larger: the cell is plainly wider than every red cell around it, up to about twice a red cell across

3. nuclear_lobation
   What is the shape of the nucleus: no nucleus at all, a single round or oval mass, a single indented, kidney-shaped or band-like mass, two lobes joined by a strand, or three or more lobes joined by thin strands?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     none: the cell carries no internal outline at all, so neither a lobe nor an indentation can be sought
     round: a single unbroken circular or oval mass with a smooth convex edge and no notch in it
     indented: one mass whose edge is notched, kidney- or band-shaped, the notch cutting less than half way across
     bilobed: two rounded segments of similar size joined by a single narrow thread of chromatin
     multilobed: three to five rounded segments strung in a chain, each joined to the next by a thin thread

4. chromatin_density
   How dense is the nuclear material: there is no nuclear material at all, open and finely textured with pale areas, moderately clumped, densely clumped but with a visible coarse pattern of light and dark, or so uniformly dark and structureless that no internal pattern can be seen at all?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     no_nucleus: the cell holds no nuclear material anywhere, so there is no interior texture to judge for density
     open_fine: the nuclear interior is mostly pale with an even fine stipple and no dark clump larger than a dot
     moderately_clumped: dark clumps cover under about half the nuclear area and the pale gaps between them stay plainly visible
     dense_clumped: coarse violet clumps cover most of the nucleus, leaving only thin pale seams between them
     pyknotic_structureless: the nucleus reads as one uniformly dark blot in which no lighter area or pattern can be made out

5. nucleus_to_cytoplasm_ratio
   About what fraction of the cell area is nucleus: none of it, because there is no nucleus, less than a third (low), between a third and two thirds (moderate), or more than two thirds (high)?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     no_nucleus: no nuclear area is present to measure, so the cell body reads as cytoplasm from edge to edge
     low: cytoplasm forms a broad rim and the nucleus covers less than a third of the cell area
     moderate: the nucleus covers between a third and two thirds of the cell, with a clear rim of cytoplasm all round
     high: the nucleus fills more than two thirds of the cell and cytoplasm survives only as a thin crescent

6. cytoplasm_basophilia
   What colour is the cytoplasm around the nucleus: pink or almost colourless, pale grey-blue, plainly blue, or deep intense blue?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     pink_or_colourless: the cytoplasm is pink or almost clear, no bluer than the red cells lying around the cell
     pale_grey_blue: a faint grey-blue wash, bluer than the red cells but much paler than the nucleus
     blue: an even mid-blue read at a glance, clearly stronger than a grey wash but lighter than the nucleus
     deep_blue: an intense navy blue nearly as dark as the nucleus, the strongest blue anywhere in the frame

7. granule_abundance
   How prominent are granules, that is small discrete dots, in the cytoplasm: none visible, fine and sparse or dust-like, or coarse and abundant so that they fill the cytoplasm?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     absent: the cytoplasm is smooth and even, showing no discrete dots against its own colour
     fine_sparse: dots are dust-like and not separately countable, so the cytoplasm looks faintly textured rather than dotted
     coarse_abundant: dots are separately countable at the cell edge and pack the cytoplasm so that little clear ground shows

8. granule_colour
   If granules are visible, what colour are they, from palest to darkest: none visible, pale lilac or neutral, orange-red, or dark purple to black?
   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     none_visible: no dots of any colour stand out from the even tint of the cytoplasm
     pale_neutral: dots are pale lilac or grey, barely different in hue from the cytoplasm carrying them
     orange_red: dots are plainly orange to red, warmer than the cytoplasm and than the violet of the nucleus
     dark_purple: dots are dark purple to nearly black, darker than the nucleus they frequently lie across

9. granules_obscure_nucleus

   Levels, in order, and what each looks like:
     absent: every part of the nuclear outline can be followed; the dots lie beside the nucleus, not across it
     partial: dots cross the nucleus and blur one stretch of its edge, while the remainder stays traceable
     marked: dots cover the nucleus so densely that no stretch of its outline can be followed at all
