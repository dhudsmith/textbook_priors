\section{The question, and what was fixed before the numbers existed}

A multimodal language model carries textbook knowledge about what pathology looks like. This study
asks what that knowledge is worth, measured in the currency a practitioner cares about: labelled
images. On the \numDatasets\ MedMNIST 2D benchmarks, a vision--language model scores each image
against a cited bank of diagnostic visual features, and five arms are compared on one shared test
sample per dataset:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A, zero-shot} --- the model's class distribution from the image and the class names.
        No labels.
  \item \textbf{B, textbook-only} --- nearest class fingerprint from the concept bank. No labels.
  \item \textbf{C, concept probe} --- regularised logistic regression on the concept scores, $n$ labels.
  \item \textbf{P, pixel probe} --- the same regression on frozen ImageNet features, the same $n$ labels.
  \item \textbf{C+P, complement} --- the same regression on both feature blocks concatenated, the
        same $n$ labels: what the textbook adds on top of the pixels (H5).
\end{itemize}

Arms C and P differ in their features and in nothing else: the same classifier, the same
regularisation search and the same labelled subsets. The five hypotheses and their decision rules
were fixed in \texttt{WORKFLOW.md} before any number existed, and are reported here as they were
written. Every across-dataset rule is a one-sided sign test held to one level, $p < \alphaLevel$:
an arm must win on at least \minWinsAll\ of the \numDatasets\ datasets, or \minWinsArmB\ of the
\numArmBDatasets\ that have an arm B. The study was decided on six datasets first and extended to
\numDatasets\ with those six verdicts known; \texttt{WORKFLOW.md} section~2 says so, and the level
reproduces the six-dataset rule exactly.

One dataset, chestmnist, is multi-label: fourteen findings that co-occur. A nearest fingerprint and
a distribution over class names are not defined over such a label space, so it has arms C and P
only, fitted one finding at a time, and counts for H1 alone; its AUC is the mean over findings.

One further comparison point was added after those results were in hand, and it decides nothing.
The \textbf{published literature} for these same tasks is read in as a fixed ceiling, so the
arms can be placed against a benchmark trained on the full training split
(Section~\ref{sec:literature}). It is an extension in the sense of \texttt{WORKFLOW.md}
section~10: no \emph{supported} verdict in this report reads it.

\section{Data and provenance}

The \numDatasets\ MedMNIST v2 224-pixel 2D benchmarks (Zenodo record 10519652), read from the
pinned release files whose names, checksums, split sizes and label maps are recorded in
\texttt{config/medmnist.yaml} and held to the installed \texttt{medmnist} package by the smoke
tier. Per dataset: a 500-image test sample shared by every arm and every model, and a 2000-image
labelled pool from the official train split, both drawn with seed~0 and both capped at the official
split where it is smaller --- breastmnist has 156 test and 546 training images, retinamnist 400 and
1080, and the learning curve stops where their pools do.

The concept bank (\texttt{data/concepts/}) was built outside the workflow by literature review,
with every feature and every class fingerprint carrying a citation. \textbf{Expert review is
simulated}: no clinician has reviewed those files, and each says so. Nothing downstream should be
read as if one had.

\textbf{A stated limit.} The test sample is random rather than stratified, so a rare class is thin:
dermamnist holds six dermatofibromas and five vascular lesions of its 500. Because the AUC is the
unweighted mean of one-vs-rest columns, such a column counts as much as one built on hundreds, and
the absolute per-dataset AUCs should be read with that in mind. The hypotheses are paired
differences on the same images, where a thin column is common mode.

\section{Methods as executed}

\subsection*{The calls, and what was archived}

Each call is one chat completion carrying a system message and a user message; the user message is
the rendered prompt text followed by the 224-pixel image as a lossless PNG data URI, encoded from
the release array without resizing. Both prompts are pure functions of the concept bank and the
pinned label map, rendered once per dataset by their own rule and hashed, so the string sent, the
string hashed into every response manifest and the string printed in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts} are one artifact. The full text of both prompts for every dataset is in
that appendix.

Calls ran at \texttt{temperature: 0} with reasoning disabled by
\texttt{chat\_template\_kwargs.enable\_thinking: false} and a budget of 512 completion tokens, in
chunks of 100 images. A reply that did not parse bought exactly one retry at a doubled budget --- the
same prompt, the same temperature, the repair being aimed at a reply cut off mid-JSON --- after
which the answer was recorded as missing and never guessed. Transport failures (429 and 5xx) were
retried separately with exponential backoff, up to four times, with a 120-second per-call timeout.
Every attempt's raw message is kept in the archive, including the ones that failed, so the record
shows what the model said and not only what could be read from it. The archive is write-protected
once written; re-querying is a deliberate act.

H4's two readers are the exception to the reasoning setting and are described with H4 below.

\subsection*{From answers to a concept vector}

A concept's ordered scale maps to equally spaced values on $[0,1]$, so a level's numeric value is
its position over one less than the number of levels. This weights concepts by scale length --- an
adjacent miss costs $1.0$ on a two-level scale and $0.25$ on a five-level one --- which is a
consequence of the mapping and not a claim about importance, and it is why no per-concept
coefficient anywhere here should be read as one. A missing answer takes the labelled pool's median
for that concept, and a binary indicator column records that it was missing, so the regression can
use ``the model would not say'' as information rather than as a value. An indicator that would be
constant carries nothing and is dropped; a concept the model never answered anywhere in the pool
has no median and takes $0.5$. The indicator columns are chosen on the pool and reused unchanged on
the test images, because the fit and the prediction must have the same columns.
Table~\ref{tab:features} gives the resulting widths per dataset.

\subsection*{The arms}

\textbf{Arm A} reads the archived zero-shot distribution straight out of the archive, one column
per class in label-index order; a class the model omitted from its JSON scores zero. No softmax is
applied and none is needed: the metric ranks each class column independently.

\textbf{Arm B} maps each class's fingerprint through the same level-to-number mapping, with
\texttt{any} masked out, and scores a class by the negative mean absolute difference over the
concepts that the fingerprint commits to \emph{and} the image answered. A class with no overlap at
all scores $-1$, the worst such a mean can be: nothing speaks for it, and leaving it undefined
would make the AUC undefined rather than merely uninformative. A thin fingerprint is therefore
scored on few concepts, and a near-miss on any of them costs it proportionally more.

\textbf{Arms C, P and C+P} are one multinomial logistic regression under three feature blocks.
Arm~C takes the concept vectors with their missing-indicator columns; arm~P takes the 512
penultimate activations of a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 (\texttt{IMAGENET1K\_V1}), with greyscale
images repeated across three channels and only the normalisation applied --- the weights' own
transform resizes to 256 and crops back to 224, which would discard the frame edge the release
already provides at the right size.

\textbf{Arm C+P fuses the two by concatenation, and by nothing else.} The concept block
(concepts plus indicators) is placed to the left of the pixel block in one design matrix, so its
width is exactly the sum of the two, which Table~\ref{tab:features} reports per dataset. That
matrix is then standardised column-wise on the $n$ labelled images --- each column centred and
scaled by its own mean and standard deviation over those rows, which is what puts a concept column
on $[0,1]$ and a ResNet activation on the same footing --- and handed to the same regression with
the same single $\ell_2$ penalty over the whole matrix, the same grid, the same cross-validation
and the same labelled subsets as arm~P. There is no per-block weighting, no per-block penalty, no
feature selection and no dimensionality reduction on either side. The point of the arm is that it
differs from arm~P in the presence of the concept columns and in nothing else, which is what makes
the difference attributable to them; the cost of that discipline is stated with H5 below.

\subsection*{The classifier, identically for C, P and C+P}

Features are standardised on the $n$ labelled images and the test images are transformed by that
same fit. The regularisation strength is chosen inside those same $n$ images by stratified
$k$-fold cross-validation, scored by mean out-of-fold accuracy, over
\texttt{scikit-learn}'s $C$ --- the \emph{inverse} penalty --- on the grid
$\{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100\}$. There is no validation set: $n$ labels means $n$ labels. The fold
count is the smaller of five and the smallest class count in the subset, and below two folds the
grid's median is taken without a search. The final fit uses lbfgs with a 2000-iteration cap and
softmax over all classes at once. A class absent from a subset gets an all-zero score column rather
than being dropped, so every arm's score matrix has the same shape and the AUC is over the same
classes; a subset containing one class only is reported as degenerate rather than given a fabricated
probability.

Labelled subsets are class-stratified nested prefixes of the pool: each subset extends the one
before it, so the curve is one labelling effort growing rather than unrelated draws at each size. Each is as
close to the pool's class proportions as whole numbers allow by largest remainder, with a floor of
one image per class, because a subset missing a class cannot predict it. Three seeds draw three
such sequences and every curve point is the mean over them.

\subsection*{The multi-label task}

chestmnist has fourteen findings that co-occur, so arms A and B are undefined for it and are not
run. Arms C, P and C+P fit the same regression one finding at a time, one-vs-rest, on the same
feature blocks. Two things differ and are stated rather than left to be discovered: the subsets are
stratified on whether a film carries any finding at all rather than on a class, and the penalty is
chosen by out-of-fold AUC rather than accuracy, because a finding present in two percent of films
makes ``always negative'' the most accurate classifier at every strength. A finding with no
positives in a subset cannot be fitted and scores a constant column, which the metric reads as
uninformative.

\subsection*{The permutation controls}

Two, both free re-analyses of the archive. Arm~B's control attaches the same fingerprint rows to
the wrong classes, so the bank still says what a textbook says and only the labels on it are
shuffled; a draw equal to the identity is rejected and redrawn. Arm~C's control shuffles each
concept column across images independently, so every concept's marginal distribution survives and
only its pairing with the image, and hence with the label, is destroyed. Each runs at three seeds
and the reported drop is the mean over them.

\subsection*{The metric, the bootstrap, and $n_B$}

The metric is \texttt{medmnist.evaluator.getAUC}: the positive column for a binary task and the
unweighted mean of the one-vs-rest columns otherwise, over classes for a multi-class task and over
findings for the multi-label one. It is computed here by an equivalent average-rank formula, which
the smoke tier holds to the package's own evaluator on every task type, including on the coarse
tied scores arm~B produces.

One bootstrap per dataset is shared by every arm: a replicate resamples the test images once with
replacement and every arm is recomputed on that same resample, so a difference between two arms is
a difference of two columns of one matrix and its interval is a percentile of that. Ten thousand
replicates, seed~0, 95\% percentile intervals. A class with no positives in a replicate is
undefined rather than counted as one half. H4's readers are scored on a prefix of the test sample
rather than all of it, so they get their own bootstrap over that prefix; two sets of images cannot
share one resample.

$n_B$ is the smallest grid point at which the pixel probe's seed-mean AUC reaches the zero-label
textbook arm. It is ordinal, not numeric: \texttt{<=50} records that the probe was already above at
the first grid point and \texttt{>2000} that it never crossed, and the median across datasets is
taken on ranks so that neither is discarded --- dropping them would remove exactly the datasets
carrying the most evidence.

\section{Results}

\subsection*{H1 --- Substitution: \hOneSupported}

\emph{Textbook features are worth a measurable number of labelled images.} Supported if the median
$n_B$ is at least 100 over the \numArmBDatasets\ arm-B datasets \textbf{and} arm C beats arm P at
the smallest labelled subset on at least \minWinsAll\ of \numDatasets\ datasets. Arm C won on
\cBeatsPWins\ of \numDatasets\ (one-sided sign test $p = \cBeatsPp$); the pixel probe never
reached the textbook arm inside the curve on \neverReached\ datasets and started above it on
\startedAbove.

\subsection*{H4 --- Reading the features better: \hFourSupported}

\emph{A better reader gets more class information out of the same images.} Two steps, each
changing exactly one thing, on the \hFourSubsample\ images every reader was scored on:
\hFourBaseline\ at no reasoning effort, the same model asked to think at \texttt{medium}
(\textbf{H4a}), and \hFourFrontier, a frontier model asked to think as hard (\textbf{H4b}).
The second step cannot be run against the no-reasoning baseline, because the frontier model cannot
be asked to stop reasoning; holding the effort fixed instead is what makes it a comparison of
models rather than of models and effort together.

The measure is a cross-validated probe on the concept answers, not arm B and not arm C. Arm B
reads the answers through the nearest-fingerprint rule, which H2 showed to be lossy, so a
comparison of readers through it would be a comparison through a broken channel; arm C would need
a labelled pool for every reader, which this study deliberately does not buy. The probe fits arm
C's classifier inside the scored images themselves by stratified cross-validation, with folds
fixed by seed and identical across readers, and every image is scored by a fit that never saw it.
It measures what the answers carry. It is not a point on the learning curve, and no $n_B$ reads it.

The readers are the one place the call settings differ from the archive's. Both were asked for
\texttt{reasoning\_effort: medium}, and where an effort is sent the thinking-off switch is not:
they are two ways of saying one thing to a stack that need not agree with itself. Their token
budgets are their own, sized from measured completion lengths --- 2048 on the local reader, where
512 is what made thinking look impossible and a thinking answer costs about 986 completion tokens
against 112 with thinking off, and 4096 on the gateway model, which is also billed at a discounted
service tier. The gateway dialect takes \texttt{max\_completion\_tokens} rather than
\texttt{max\_tokens}, which is why the two readers are two rules.

Two limits on H4b. The frontier model is closed and of unknown size, so the step is capability and
not parameters. And it refuses \texttt{temperature: 0} --- only its served default is allowed ---
so it is the one reader here whose answers are sampled rather than deterministic; they would differ
if re-bought, and the paired bootstrap cannot see that, because it resamples images over answers
that are fixed only in the archive.

H4a won on \hFourAWins\ of \numArmBDatasets\ datasets and H4b on \hFourBWins\ of
\numArmBDatasets; both are held to the level every rule is held to, at least \minWinsArmB\ of
\numArmBDatasets. All \numReaders\ readers are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:h4} and Figure~\ref{fig:readers}, not only the three the chain reads.

\subsection*{H5 --- Complement: \hFiveSupported}

\emph{The textbook adds something the pixels do not already carry.} H1 asks whether the concept
scores can replace the pixel features at equal labels and answers no; that is not the same as
asking whether they carry anything the pixels lack. Arm C+P gives the same regularised regression
both feature blocks at once: the concept block concatenated to the left of the pixel block in one
design matrix, standardised column-wise on the same $n$ labelled images and fitted with the same
single $\ell_2$ penalty, the same grid and the same nested subsets as arm~P, with no per-block
weighting and no feature selection. The fusion is described in full under Methods and its widths
are in Table~\ref{tab:features}; arm C+P differs from arm~P in the presence of the concept columns
and in nothing else. Supported if it beats arm~P at $n = \hFiveN$ on at least \minWinsAll\ of
\numDatasets\ datasets.

Arm C+P won on \hFiveWins\ of \numDatasets\ ($p = \hFivep$), with a median difference of
\hFiveMedianGain\ AUC; \hFiveClearGains\ datasets have an interval clear of zero above and
\hFiveClearLosses\ below. The rule was written into \texttt{WORKFLOW.md} and
\texttt{config/config.yaml} and committed before arm C+P was fitted for the first time, which is
what separates it from an arm chosen after seeing what would win. It costs no calls: both feature
blocks were already on disk, so the whole comparison is a re-analysis of the existing archive.

One limit, stated rather than tuned away: seven to fourteen concept columns join 512 pixel columns
under a single penalty (Table~\ref{tab:features}), so the concept block is outnumbered by roughly
forty to one and a real but small contribution can be shrunk away. A null here would mean no
detectable gain under the classifier every other arm uses, not that the concept answers carry
nothing --- Table~\ref{tab:h2}'s permutation controls show they carry class information. The same
asymmetry cuts the other way for a positive result: a gain found under this handicap is not an
artefact of giving the concept block room it was not entitled to.

\section{Extension: reading the arms against the published literature}
\label{sec:literature}

Everything in this section is a fixed comparison point, not a computation, and it decides no
hypothesis. \texttt{data/literature/benchmarks.yaml} pins the AUC and ACC that
\cite{yang2023} report for five fully supervised methods on these same \numDatasets\ MedMNIST v2 tasks at
the same 224-pixel resolution --- trained on each dataset's whole official training split,
thousands to tens of thousands of images, not this study's $n \leq 2000$ labelled pool. Table~%
\ref{tab:literature} sets every arm of this study beside the best of those five published numbers,
so a reader can see how far a handful of labels and a textbook prior sit from a benchmark trained
at full scale, without mistaking the gap for a finding about any arm here.

Read that way, the gap tracks the label budget and little else. The best zero-label arm on each
dataset --- whichever of A and B wins there --- sits a median \litGapZeroMedian\ AUC below the
published best, and is within five points of it on \litZeroWithinFivePoints\ of \numArmBDatasets\
datasets. Buying \litLargestN\ labels (or a dataset's whole pool where that is smaller) closes most
of what remains: the pixel probe's median gap is
\litGapPixelMedian, within two points of the ceiling on \litPixelWithinTwoPoints\ of \numDatasets\
and at or above it on \litPixelAtOrAboveCeiling, while the concept probe at the same budget sits
\litGapConceptMedian\ below it. The ordering is the
same one H1 reports --- pixels with labels beat the textbook without them --- now with an external
scale attached to it.

Two cautions on reading the table. The published methods see each dataset's whole official training
split, so none of this is a same-conditions comparison, and the gaps above are not differences any
bootstrap here covers: they subtract a number from another paper, with no interval. And the last
column is the best of five methods per dataset, which is not always the same method; the figure
instead draws the ResNet-18 (224) row alone, the one backbone trained at this study's own input
resolution, as a finely dotted line on Figure~\ref{fig:curve}. Table~3 of \cite{yang2023} reports
ACC beside AUC and \texttt{benchmarks.yaml} pins both, but only AUC appears here: this study
computes no ACC for any arm, and a published ACC with nothing to set beside it would be decoration.

\section{What this study cannot separate}

Every source dataset is public and labelled, so ``the model carries textbook knowledge'' and ``the
model has seen this benchmark'' are not distinguishable with these data. Not claimed: that the
concept scores are clinically valid, that the simulated review substitutes for a clinician, or that
any arm is state of the art.
